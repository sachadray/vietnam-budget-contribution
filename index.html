    Inside the state sector

    STATE 100: the hundred largest state-owned taxpayers

    CafeF publishes a separate ranking of state-owned enterprises, on the same financial year. It ranks parent companies and their larger subsidiaries in the same hundred slots, so the headline contribution is not the sum of the rows.

    Getting from the ranking to the contribution

    Thirty-one of the hundred entries are subsidiaries of another entry. Their payments already sit inside the parent's figure, so they are netted out once.

    Published figure: ₫425,800 bn, about 16% of national revenue. The ₫35 bn difference against the row arithmetic is rounding in the source.

    How this was put together

    Sources

    CafeF labels each list by its publication year, not the year of the payments. The lists published in 2026 measure amounts remitted in financial year 2025, and those are the ones used here. PetroVietnam is ₫90,991 bn in both, which is how the convention was confirmed.

    Classifying ownership

    State-owned means the state holds control, central or provincial, including state-controlled joint ventures. Firms appearing on neither the STATE 100 nor the PRIVATE 100 were classified by hand; each override is documented in the accompanying workbook.

    Calls worth knowing about

    • Vietsovpetro counted as state: PetroVietnam holds 51%.
    • Sabeco counted as foreign: ThaiBev holds 53.6% while the ministry retains 36%.
    • Nghi Son Refinery counted as foreign: Idemitsu and KPI hold 70.2% against PetroVietnam's 25.1%.

    What this is not

    • Not a census. Both lists are cut-offs, the STATE 100 at ₫468 bn.
    • Not tax incidence. Remittance bundles taxes the firm bears with value added tax and withheld income tax it collects on the state's behalf.
    • Not audited accounts. These are publisher figures, reconciled against the publisher's own published totals but not against Ministry of Finance returns.
